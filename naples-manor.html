What Causes Clogged Drains in Naples Manor?

Naples Manor is an established residential neighborhood in East Naples, situated just south of US-41 (Tamiami Trail East) and bordered by Rattlesnake Hammock Road and Collier Boulevard. Established in the 1960s and 1970s, it consists of single-family concrete block homes, duplexes, and quiet residential streets.

Homes in Naples Manor were primarily constructed between the late 1960s and early 1980s. Underground plumbing features original PVC or early copper and galvanized branch lines beneath concrete slab floors. Decades of heavy household usage—cooking grease from large family meals, daily laundry cycles, and hair accumulation in bathtubs—gradually restrict interior pipe diameters. In addition, exterior sewer laterals often suffer from settling in sandy soil, causing low spots where toilet paper accumulates into persistent blockages.

Landscape and Root Intrusion: Mature shade trees common in Naples Manor yards—including live oaks, mango trees, avocado trees, and invasive Brazilian pepper—have extensive shallow root networks that infiltrate older pipe joints seeking water.

Utility Infrastructure: Naples Manor is connected to the Collier County Water-Sewer District municipal wastewater utility. Most homes have a ground cleanout cap located in the front flower bed or along the exterior foundation wall.

Why does my toilet gurgle when someone takes a shower or runs laundry?

Gurgling sounds indicate your main sewer drain is partially choked or your plumbing vent stack is obstructed. Air cannot escape freely, so it bubbles back up through the nearest plumbing trap. We snake the main line to restore unrestricted airflow.

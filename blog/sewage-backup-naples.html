Do These 6 Things First

  1. Stop all water use. No flushing, showers, laundry, or dishwasher. Do not run water to flush it through; every drop you add goes to the same blocked line and makes the backup worse.
  2. Keep children and pets out of the area. Never touch standing sewage with bare hands; sewage carries dangerous bacteria and contaminants.
  3. Watch for sewer gas. If the rotten-egg sewer odor is strong or you feel dizzy, ventilate the area and leave the house immediately.
  4. Stay out of electrical hazards. Stay out of any wet area where sewage has reached outlets, extension cords, or appliances.
  5. Do not use chemical drain cleaners. They will not clear a main line backup and can corrode and damage pipes.
  6. Take photos and make the right call. Take clear photos and video before any cleanup for your insurance company, then follow the contact guide below.

Why Should You Avoid DIY Methods for a Main Line Backup?

Do not attempt DIY clearing on a main sewer backup because store-bought tools can worsen the spill and crack fragile pipes. Main line backups carry hazardous blackwater that requires professional safety gear and commercial-grade machinery.

  • Do not use a plunger: Plunging a main line backup builds air pressure that forces raw sewage out of nearby low fixtures like showers and floor drains.
  • Do not use small home cable machines: Handheld consumer snakes are too short and narrow to clear main lines, and they can easily kink, snap, or become stuck deep inside your lateral pipe.
  • Do not pour chemical drain cleaners: Harsh chemicals pool in standing sewage without reaching the obstruction, releasing hazardous fumes and eating away at older Naples cast iron pipes. For individual fixture clogs that are not part of a whole-house backup, consider professional clogged drain clearing instead.

Is It Your Pipe or the Street Main?

Look at what is backing up. One tub or one floor drain points to your own line. Several drains at once, or sewage in the lowest drain in the house, points to the main line.

Many Naples homes sit on a slab with no basement, so the lowest drain is usually a ground floor shower, tub, or floor drain. If your neighbors have the same problem, tell the utility right away. If the blockage is in the public main, the utility fixes it. If it is in your own line, a plumber or drain pro clears it using drain snaking, hydro jetting, or comprehensive sewer line cleaning.

Where is your main sewer cleanout?

Most homes have a capped cleanout pipe outside, usually near the front foundation. Only open it if you can do it safely, wearing gloves and boots, because sewage may come out. If you are not sure, do not open it. Call us.

Should You Use Snaking vs Hydro Jetting for a Main Sewer Line?

Drain snaking punches a quick hole through solid clogs to get water flowing, while hydro jetting scours the entire interior pipe wall completely clean. The right method depends on the material causing the clog and the age and condition of your sewer pipe.

  • When snaking is used: Mechanical drain snaking and cabling uses steel blades to break up heavy paper clogs, retrieve foreign objects, and chop through light root strands to restore immediate flow.
  • When hydro jetting is used: High-pressure hydro jetting uses specialized water nozzles to blast away thick cooking grease, sand, sludge, and scale deposits, restoring the line to its full original diameter. For heavy recurring backups, comprehensive sewer line cleaning provides a long-lasting scour without damaging sound pipes.

When Is Drain Cleaning Not Enough to Fix the Problem?

Drain cleaning clears soft blockages, but it cannot fix structural pipe failures like crushed sections, major sags, or broken joints. A video camera inspection reveals whether your line requires physical pipe repair instead of repeated clearings.

  • Heavy root intrusion: Invasive ficus or palm roots that shatter pipe joints will regrow and catch debris within weeks unless the joint is repaired.
  • Pipe sags or bellies: Shifting Florida sandy soils can allow pipe sections to sink, creating a low spot that collects standing water and sludge where clearing only provides temporary relief.
  • Cracked, offset, or collapsed joints: Broken clay or corroded cast iron pipes create physical ledges that snag solid waste, requiring professional drain pipe repair or trenchless replacement.

Why Sewage Backs Up in Naples

  • Grease. Collier County Public Utilities says fats, oil, and grease cause most sewer blockages.
  • Tree roots. Ficus and palm roots can push into joints in older clay and cast iron pipe.
  • A sag or broken pipe. A low spot holds waste in one place, and a cracked joint catches it.
  • Storms. Heavy rain and storm surge can overload sewers and knock out power to pump stations.

Naples has seen it. After Hurricane Irma in 2017, Collier County told residents not to use dishwashers or clothes washers because sewers were overflowing across the county. After Hurricane Ian in 2022, storm surge and flooding caused heavy damage to City of Naples sewer pumps.

During a storm, use as little water as you can. Skip extra flushes, laundry, and the dishwasher.

Why Should You Have Drains Checked After a Naples Storm?

Tropical storms, hurricanes, and king tides can push heavy sand, silt, and flood debris straight into exterior cleanouts and yard lines. Sand does not dissolve or wash away easily, so call for a camera check before running full household water again.

How to Prevent Future Sewage Backups

Taking a few proactive measures protects your plumbing system and prevents costly wastewater damage:

  • Never pour grease down the drain: Dispose of cooking fats, oils, and grease in the trash, never into kitchen sinks or garbage disposals.
  • Flush only toilet paper: Wipes (even those marketed as flushable), paper towels, feminine hygiene products, and dental floss do not disintegrate and cause severe clogs.
  • Manage invasive tree roots: Keep invasive ficus, banyan, and palm roots away from buried sewer laterals, or schedule routine maintenance.
  • Schedule camera inspections: Periodic video pipe inspections identify scale buildup, root intrusion, and pipe sags long before a full blockage occurs.
  • Install a backwater valve: A sewer backwater valve allows wastewater to exit your home but closes automatically if city sewer lines surge backward.

Cleaning Up After a Sewage Backup

The Florida Department of Health gives these steps for sewage inside a home:

  1. Keep children and pets out of the area.
  2. Wear rubber boots and waterproof gloves.
  3. Wash hard surfaces and floors with soap and water. Then disinfect with 1/4 cup of bleach in 1 gallon of water.
  4. Dry everything out to stop mold.
  5. Never mix bleach with ammonia. It makes toxic fumes.
  6. Throw away items you cannot wash or dry clean, such as mattresses, carpet, and upholstered furniture.

For a large backup, or sewage in carpet or drywall, call a cleanup company.

Does Insurance Cover a Sewage Backup?

Many Florida homeowners policies leave out damage from sewer or drain backups unless you add optional water backup coverage. Note that standard flood insurance (such as FEMA policies) only covers rising external storm surge or flooding, which is separate from interior sewer backup endorsements. What Factors Affect the Cost of Drain Cleaning in Naples?

The cost of drain cleaning depends on the location of the clog, how easy the cleanout is to reach, and the type of equipment needed to clear the line safely. We evaluate your system on-site and provide an exact quote before any work begins.

  • Location and depth of the clog: A blockage located deep near the city sewer connection requires longer reach and more labor than one right beside your cleanout.
  • Cleanout accessibility: Ground-level cleanouts that are easily accessible reduce labor compared to jobs requiring toilet removal or roof vent access.
  • Type of obstruction: Simple toilet paper clogs clear faster than heavy root intrusion, thick hardened grease, or sand packed by storm surges.
  • Equipment required: Basic mechanical cabling requires different machinery and setup time than high-pressure hydro jetting or mechanical descaling equipment.
  • Pipe age and material: Older, delicate cast iron lines require extra care, specialized cutting heads, and camera verification compared to modern PVC.

Sewage Backup FAQs

Should I call the county or a plumber first?

If you are on county sewer, Collier County says to call its 24-hour wastewater line first, before a private plumber. In the City of Naples, call the city utilities emergency line. If the utility says the main is clear, call a plumber for your own line.

Is a sewage backup dangerous?

Yes. Treat it as a health risk. Keep children and pets away, and wear rubber boots and waterproof gloves for any cleanup. If sewer gas smells are strong or you feel dizzy, leave the house immediately.

Can I flush the toilet during a storm?

Use as little water as you can. In 2017 Collier County asked residents to avoid dishwashers and clothes washers because sewers were overflowing.

Can I clean up sewage myself?

For a small spill on hard floors, follow the Florida Department of Health steps above. For a large spill, or sewage in carpet or drywall, call a cleanup company.

Should I use a plunger or snake on a main line backup?

No. Using a plunger or small home snake on a main line backup can force sewage out of lower tubs and showers or crack aging pipes. Main line backups require commercial equipment operated from an exterior cleanout by a professional.

Will the plumber need a camera?

Yes. A high-resolution sewer camera allows the technician to find the exact cause on screen, verify that the obstruction is completely cleared, and check for hidden pipe cracks or sags.

How do I know if the clog will come back?

A video camera inspection after clearing shows whether your pipe is structurally sound or has root intrusion, sags, or broken joints that will cause clogs to return. If the pipe is undamaged, practicing good drain habits will keep it flowing freely.
